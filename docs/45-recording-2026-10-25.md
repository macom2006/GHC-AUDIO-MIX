**Most stream viewers are on a phone speaker, which is mono.** Losing 3.7 dB of
the 200–500 Hz band is losing the body of the mix.

Band-by-band correlation shows why: bass correlates at +0.87 and the highs at
+0.78, but **200 Hz–1 kHz correlates at only +0.09.** In a normal mix the
midrange is the *most* correlated band, because that is where centred sources
live. Here it is nearly decorrelated.

#### Cause, in order of how fixable it is

1. **Bus 7 BROADCAST is set to width 110.** Over 100 is active M/S widening —
   it boosts the side signal, which is precisely what cancels in mono. This is
   the one to fix: **width 110 → 100**. A 10% widening is not something anyone
   will miss on headphones; 3.7 dB of missing body on every phone is.
2. **Keys 1 and Keys 2 are both stereo channels (mode ST) at width 100.** A wide
   stereo keyboard patch sits exactly in 200–500 Hz and is decorrelated by
   design. In this pad-dominated clip it dominates that band. Narrowing the keys
   width is a tonal decision for the band, not a console error.
3. **Crowd mics are hard-panned L−100 / R+100** — a spaced pair, inherently
   decorrelated. At −12 dB into the stream their contribution is limited, and
   room width in a stream is usually wanted.

Width is geometry, not taste: the mono loss can be calculated without hearing
the room. Item 1 is safe to change now; items 2 and 3 are not.

# Correction: the width change does not fix the mono loss

I proposed Bus 7 width 110 → 100 as the fix for the 3.7 dB mono cancellation.
**That was wrong, and the arithmetic says so plainly.**

Width is M/S scaling. A WING's width 0 collapses a bus to mono, which is only
true of this form:

```
L = M + k·S        R = M − k·S        k = width / 100
```

The mono sum a phone makes is `(L + R) / 2`, and:

```
(L + R)/2 = ((M + k·S) + (M − k·S)) / 2 = M
```

**`k` cancels.** The mono signal is independent of the width setting — at any
width, anywhere in the chain.

Verified on the recording itself by simulating width 110 → 100 and re-summing:

```
max |mono_at_110 − mono_at_100| = 6.9e-18      (floating-point zero)
```

| | width 110 | width 100 | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| stereo energy, 200–500 Hz | −30.1 dB | −30.5 dB | −0.5 |
| **mono energy, 200–500 Hz** | **−33.8 dB** | **−33.8 dB** | **0.0** |
| side energy, full band | −33.8 dB | −34.6 dB | −0.8 |

The measured *gap* narrows from 3.7 dB to 3.2 dB — but only because the stereo
figure came down, not because the mono figure went up. **A phone listener hears
bit-identical audio either way.**

## What V18 is actually worth

`console/GHC BROADCAST V18.snap` — one value, `/bus/7/wid: 110 → 100`.

It is a legitimate tidy-up on its own merits, and nothing more:

- Stereo and mono listeners get a more consistent mix (gap 1.9 → 1.6 dB
  full band)
- 0.83 dB less side energy passing through the ECL33, tape and limiter — those
  stages stop working on content a mono listener never hears
- Width above 100 on a broadcast bus is not good practice

It is **not** a fix for the mono problem. If the only reason to load it was to
recover the body on phones, do not bother.

## Where the mono loss actually comes from, and why no console setting fixes it

Content that cancels in mono is, by definition, side-only. It exists in `S` and
not in `M`. No gain, pan or width operation can move it into `M` — those
operations scale `M` and `S`, they do not convert between them. Setting the keys
to width 0 does not help either: it discards their side content rather than
folding it in, and `M` is unchanged.

**This is a source problem at the keyboard.** The patch Keys 1 and Keys 2 are
running generates phase-opposed content in 200–500 Hz — common in wide "stereo
pad" presets, which often use a short inverted delay or detuned anti-phase layer
to sound big. On a phone it disappears.

Real fixes, all off the console:

1. **Have the keys player choose a patch without the stereo widening** — most
   workstations offer a mono or narrow variant of the same pad.
2. **Take a single output from the keyboard** instead of a stereo pair, so what
   you get is one real channel rather than two phase-related ones.
3. **Check the mix in mono before the stream goes out** — this is the habit that
   catches it. The WING can sum the monitor bus to mono for exactly this.

Until one of those happens, the pad will sound thin on phones no matter what the
console does.

## Note on scope

The 3.7 dB figure comes from a 25-second pad-dominated clip, where the keys are
most of the signal. **In a full-band passage the proportion will be smaller**,
because drums, bass and vocals are centred and correlated. The problem is real
but its size across a whole service is not yet known — another reason for the
full-band recording.
